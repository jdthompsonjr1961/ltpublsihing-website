Author help

Do you need a book formatter? When to hire help—and when not to.

Not every author needs paid formatting help. Some books are simple enough to prepare with care and patience. Others are better handed to someone who understands print production before the upload process turns into a week of avoidable frustration.

You may not need help for a simple text-only book.

A straightforward novel or memoir with no images, no complex front matter, no tables, no footnotes, no unusual typography, and no full-bleed elements can often be formatted by a careful author using a good template.

If you are comfortable checking margins, page size, styles, page numbering, chapter starts, and export settings, you may be able to produce a perfectly acceptable file yourself.

You probably need help when the book has visual complexity.

Illustrated interiors, maps, photographs, poetry, recipes, tables, appendices, art pages, ornaments, wide images, or full-bleed pages increase the production risk. These books are less forgiving because design choices interact with trim, bleed, margins, and print resolution.

A book with one or two images may still be simple. A book where image placement is part of the reading experience usually needs more deliberate production.

You may need help if the cover is a full-wrap print file.

An ebook cover is a front image. A paperback or hardcover cover is a mechanical wrap: back cover, spine, front cover, bleed, barcode area, trim lines, fold lines, and sometimes hinge areas. It must fit the final page count and paper choice.

If you are guessing at spine width or placing the design over a template without understanding the safe zones, professional help can save repeated rejection.

You need help when fixing one error creates another.

One KDP warning is often manageable. A chain of warnings is different. If correcting bleed creates margin errors, or changing page size breaks the cover, or a corrected PDF creates font problems, the file likely has a structural issue.

At that point, paying for a narrow repair may be cheaper than spending days rebuilding the same file without knowing which assumption is wrong.

Do not hire someone who turns every problem into a package.

A targeted file problem should not automatically become a full publishing package. A rejected cover may need a cover rebuild. A gutter warning may need an interior correction. A manuscript that is not ready may need editorial work before formatting.

The right service begins with diagnosis. The answer might be “this is a quick fix,” “this needs a partial rebuild,” or “the book is not ready for production yet.”

Hiring production help should not affect your rights.

A formatter, designer, or file-fix service should not require you to surrender publishing rights to solve a production problem. Paid production help is separate from imprint publication, distribution, or editorial acceptance.

At LT Publishing, file repair and book-production services do not make us your publisher unless there is a separate imprint agreement. You remain free to publish independently or place the book elsewhere.
